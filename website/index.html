File 01 How this came to be

I have a Kindle Paperwhite from 2012. The screen is perfect, the battery lasts weeks, and in all those years it has never once tried to sell me something I didn't ask for.

On May 20, 2026, Amazon decided it was done with it. Every Kindle made in 2012 or earlier lost the store: no buying, no borrowing, no downloading. The books already on it could stay. Everything else was gone.

The device still worked. It just wasn't allowed to get anything new.

So I jailbroke it. That part is easy now; the Kindle Modding Wiki walks you through it. What wasn't easy was everything after: Calibre on a laptop, a cable, an SFTP client, shell commands, PDFs that sliced a line in half at the bottom of every page, and books named OD4388757722 with no author and a grey square for a cover.

Reading had become an IT job.

PageDrop is what I built so it stops being one. Type a title on your phone. Tap send. It's on the Kindle across the room before you've put the phone down.

— Written on a phone, read on a Kindle Amazon gave up on

File 02 What it solves

Six things that made reading a chore.

  1. 01
    The store is gone

    Search, then send

    Find books from Project Gutenberg, Standard Ebooks, the Internet Archive and your own catalogs. Pick one; it arrives in the best format your Kindle can read.

  2. 02
    Cables and Calibre

    Your phone is the whole toolchain

    Everything travels over Wi-Fi through KOReader's SSH server. Books land on the Kindle's home screen and in KOReader at the same time.

  3. 03
    PDFs that cut lines off

    PDFs that read like books

    Hard line breaks unwrapped, running headers and page numbers stripped, and paragraphs rejoined across pages, instead of a scan pretending to be a book.

  4. 04
    “Unknown Author”, no cover

    Books that know their names

    Title, author and cover come from the file itself and from open catalogs, then get written into the book, so the Kindle shows them properly too.

  5. 05
    What's even on it?

    The Kindle, from your pocket

    See every book on the device, delete what you've finished, and save Kindle-only books back to your phone.

  6. 06
    Where do I even start

    A setup that holds your hand

    A step-by-step checklist sends you to the Kindle Modding Wiki for the jailbreak, then handles KOReader, updates and Wi-Fi with you.

File 03 How it works

Three steps. One of them, once.

Get your Kindle ready

Jailbreak, install KOReader, switch on its SSH server. PageDrop walks you through it and finds your Kindle on the network.

Find a book

Type a title. Or share a file to PageDrop from anywhere on your phone.

It's on your Kindle

Converted if it needs to be, named and covered properly, sent over Wi-Fi. Done.

File 05 The fine print

You'll need

An Android phone and a jailbroken Kindle running KOReader, on the same Wi-Fi. Built and tested on a 2012 Paperwhite; any jailbroken Kindle with KOReader should work.

Your books stay yours

No sign-up, no tracking. Books convert on your phone; an optional conversion server can help with stubborn files, and one switch turns it off.

Open source

GPL-3.0. Read it, fork it, fix it on GitHub. Jailbreak guides live on the Kindle Modding Wiki.
